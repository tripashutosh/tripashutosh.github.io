About

I am a postdoctoral researcher at the Asia Pacific Center for Theoretical Physics (APCTP) in Pohang, working with Dr. Hyun-Sik Jeong. I received my PhD in 2025 from SOKENDAI, carrying out my research at the KEK Theory Center in Japan under Prof. Jun Nishimura. Before that, I completed an integrated BS-MS in Physics at IISER Mohali, with my Master's thesis under Dr. Anosh Joseph.

My research focuses on non-perturbative problems in quantum field theory, lattice gauge theory, and matrix models motivated by string theory and quantum gravity. I am particularly interested in computational obstacles such as the sign problem, topological freezing, and large-N scaling, and in methods that get around them: complexified path integrals and Lefschetz thimbles, quantum algorithms such as sample-based Krylov quantum diagonalization, and physics-informed neural networks for holographic QCD. My broader goal is to build computational frameworks that connect high-energy theory with modern quantum computing and machine learning.

Research

Lefschetz thimbles, the sign problem and matrix models

Monte Carlo simulation of theories with complex actions, using the generalized Lefschetz thimble method. I applied it to the Lorentzian type IIB (IKKT) matrix model, a non-perturbative proposal for superstring theory, and showed that the Euclidean and Lorentzian versions are not equivalent. I am now extending these methods to 2D U(1) lattice gauge theory with a θ term, where the sign problem and topological freezing appear together.

PRL 134, 211601 (2025) · PhD thesis · manuscript in preparation

Quantum algorithms for matrix quantum mechanics

Ground states of gauged matrix models on quantum hardware. With M. Hanada and H.-S. Jeong, I applied sample-based Krylov quantum diagonalization to the bosonic two-matrix model, which shares the commutator-squared interaction of the BFSS and BMN models, and ran it on IBM superconducting processors.

Manuscript in preparation · APCTP Focus Program 2026 · QuantHEP 2026

Machine learning for holography

Physics-informed neural networks for the holographic inverse problem: reconstructing a gravity dual, including higher-curvature couplings, directly from lattice-QCD thermodynamics and the transport properties of the quark-gluon plasma.

Manuscript in preparation · talks at KIAS, SIMIS and Holography & Quantum Matter 2026
